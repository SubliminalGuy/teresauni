Biography

Dr. Teresa Serrano Martín is one of the most distinguished archaeologists of her generation. Born in Salamanca, Spain, she developed an early passion for the human past growing up near the medieval city walls and the treasures of the University of Salamanca, one of Europe's oldest seats of learning. That formative environment — where Roman foundations lie beneath medieval stone — instilled in her a lifelong conviction that the layers of the past are not dead matter but living testimony.

She completed her undergraduate degree at the Universidad Complutense de Madrid, graduating first in her class with a licenciatura in Prehistoric Archaeology in 1991. She earned her Doctorate at the University of Oxford in 1996, under the supervision of Professor Sir Barry Cunliffe, with a thesis entitled "Settlement Hierarchies and Exchange Networks in Late Bronze Age Iberia: New Evidence from the Meseta Central." The thesis was awarded the Haverfield Prize and subsequently published by Oxford University Press to widespread critical acclaim.

Following postdoctoral fellowships at the École Normale Supérieure (Paris) and the Deutsches Archäologisches Institut (Berlin), Dr. Serrano Martín joined the faculty of Teresais University in 2001 as a Lecturer in Iberian Prehistory. She was appointed to her chair in 2008 and assumed the Directorship of the University in 2014 — the first woman to hold that position in the institution's history.

Research Interests

Dr. Serrano Martín's scholarship spans four decades of fieldwork and archival research. Her primary interests include:

  • Late Bronze Age and Early Iron Age settlement patterns in the Iberian Peninsula
  • Intraregional exchange networks and the emergence of proto-urban complexity
  • Archaeometallurgy and the social organisation of bronze production
  • Mortuary practices and social stratification in prehistoric Hispania
  • Digital methods in landscape archaeology: LiDAR, GIS, and photogrammetry
  • Heritage ethics and the politics of archaeological interpretation

Her current flagship project, The Iberian Bronze Age Project (IBAP), is a ten-year collaborative excavation at the Serra d'Atapuerca region. Funded by an ERC Advanced Grant of €3.2 million, IBAP has already produced evidence of a previously unknown fortified settlement dating to c. 1800 BCE, challenging long-held models of Bronze Age social complexity in the western Mediterranean.

Teaching

Despite her administrative responsibilities as Director, Dr. Serrano Martín remains a committed and celebrated teacher. She currently convenes two graduate seminars:

  • ARCH 700 — Field School: Eastern Mediterranean (Doctoral, Summer Term)
  • ARCH 801 — Landscape Archaeology: Theory and Method (Masters/Doctoral, Spring Term)

She has supervised 34 doctoral students to completion, many of whom hold chairs at leading universities worldwide. Former students consistently describe her seminars as among the most formative intellectual experiences of their careers.

Media & Public Engagement

Dr. Serrano Martín is a prominent voice in the public communication of science and archaeology. She has presented three BBC documentary series, including Beneath Iberia (BBC Four, 2021), and writes a regular column for El País on heritage, memory, and the politics of the past. She has delivered keynote addresses at the World Archaeological Congress, the European Association of Archaeologists, and TEDxMadrid.
